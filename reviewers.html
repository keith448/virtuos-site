VirtuOS

Review for VirtuOS

First pilot: reciprocity, 30 entries, three to four hours.

VirtuOS records how good people act across traditions and turns the record into a benchmark for models. Each entry has four parts: the situation, what the wise person did or said, why it worked, and the tempting wrong answer. A virtue enters the shared core only when five lineages reached it and reviewers outside each lineage agree.

We need six reviewers for the first pilot. Your labels are the evidence.

The task

  • Read one entry at a time in a blind page. You see the layer, the language, the source title, the four parts, and one virtue.
  • Answer one question: does this entry teach that virtue as its lesson, with the tempting wrong answer as its opposite?
  • Agree or disagree. A disagreement needs one or two sentences of reason.
  • Eight minutes an entry. Thirty entries is three to four hours over two weeks.
  • You never edit an entry. Your labels are the evidence.

You will not see the tradition, the lineage root, the entry's status, the virtue's strength, the other virtues, or anyone else's labels. The server checks whether you sit outside the entry's lineage and shows only a flag.

How the work is used in AI development

The labels are the evidence. They decide whether a virtue counts as shared across traditions, and that count is what makes the benchmark credible. We publish the count with the entries, the sources and the reviewers' disciplines, and we are packaging the entries as an installable test that AI developers run against a model. The hope is that a model which handles these situations makes better decisions when it is deployed. That is the hypothesis the pilot tests, and we report the result even if it does not hold. The data is CC BY-SA.

Who we need

Six people who between them cover these roots: Islamic, Confucian, Greek and Roman, Rabbinic Judaism, South Asian religions, and one folk tradition. Each reviewer labels the entries outside their own root, so a slate that spans roots gives every entry four or five eligible readers.

Credentials

You need one of these: reading competence in the source language of the material you review, or documented formation in the tradition (ordination, a graduate degree in the field, or five or more years teaching in the tradition). You should be able to point to a CV line, a publication, a translation, or an institutional or community role we can check.

Two rules decide whether a label counts. You cannot be the entry's author. You must sit outside the entry's lineage: if any of your traditions shares the entry's root, we store your label and it does not count. A second, unrelated tradition does not buy independence.

Time and pay

Eight minutes a label, 30 entries in the first batch. The reciprocity batch is paid at US$75 an hour; scarce language specialists can be paid more. Contractor terms, paid within 14 days of invoice. One in twenty labels is read by a second qualified reviewer.

What happens after you apply

  1. A curator reads the form within five working days.
  2. If it fits, a 30-minute call, then a 20-item calibration set keyed to the codebook. Eighty percent concordance means you apply the codebook as it is currently read.
  3. You get the live queue: 30 entries, blind, one judgment at a time.
  4. You can stop after the first batch. Continued review is optional.

Data and consent

We store your application and your labels. Your name and discipline are recorded with each label and may be published with the release; the label is the evidence. We do not publish your email. You can withdraw and ask us to delete your data before a release is frozen.
