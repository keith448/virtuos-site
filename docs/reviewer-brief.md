# Reviewer brief — reciprocity pilot

VirtuOS records how good people act across traditions and turns the record into a
benchmark for models. Each entry has four parts: the situation, what the wise
person did or said, why it worked, and the tempting wrong answer. A virtue enters
the shared core only when five lineages reached it and reviewers outside each
lineage agree.

We are recruiting six reviewers for the first pilot: reciprocity, 30 entries.

## The task

- Read one entry at a time in a blind page. You see the layer, the language, the
  source title, the four parts, and one virtue.
- Answer one question. Does this entry teach that virtue as its lesson, with the
  tempting wrong answer as its opposite?
- Agree or disagree. A disagreement needs one or two sentences of reason.
- Eight minutes an entry. Thirty entries is two to three hours, spread over two
  weeks.
- You never edit an entry. Your labels are the evidence.

You will not see the tradition, the lineage root, the entry's status, the virtue's
strength, the other virtues, or anyone else's labels. The server checks whether
you sit outside the entry's lineage and shows only a flag.

## Who we need

Six people who between them cover these roots: Islamic, Confucian, Greek and
Roman, Rabbinic Judaism, South Asian religions, and one folk tradition. Each
reviewer labels the entries outside their own root, so a slate that spans roots
gives every entry four or five eligible readers.

## Credentials

You need one of these:

- Reading competence in the source language of the material you review.
- Documented formation in the tradition: ordination, a graduate degree in the
  field, or five or more years teaching in the tradition.

You should be able to point to a CV line, a publication, a translation, or an
institutional or community role we can check. Record your discipline: scripture,
folklore, film, sport coaching, interfaith, anthropology, philosophy, or other.

Two rules decide whether a label counts. You cannot be the entry's author. You
must sit outside the entry's lineage: if any of your traditions shares the
entry's root, we store your label and it does not count toward reach. Holding a
second, unrelated tradition does not buy independence.

## Time and pay

- Eight minutes a label. First batch: 30 entries.
- US$40 to US$75 an hour for general readers; US$100 to US$180 for scarce
  language specialists. Paid as a contractor within 14 days of invoice.
- One in twenty labels is read by a second qualified reviewer.

## What happens after you apply

1. You send the form. It takes about two minutes.
2. A curator reads it within five working days.
3. If it fits, a 30-minute call, then a 20-item calibration set keyed to the
   codebook. Eighty percent concordance means you apply the codebook as it is
   currently read.
4. You get the live queue: 30 entries, blind, one judgment at a time.
5. You can stop after the first batch. Continued review is optional.

## Data and consent

We store your application and your labels. Your name and discipline are recorded
with each label and may be published with the release; the label is the evidence.
We do not publish your email. You can withdraw and ask us to delete your data
before a release is frozen.

## What we ask you not to do

- Do not edit entries. The curator owns the text; reviewers own the labels.
- Do not use a model to make the judgment. The account records that the reviewer
  is a person; a model label is rejected by the database.
- Do not share the blind page or the queue contents before the release.
